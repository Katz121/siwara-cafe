Then · Before · Now

Then

Wat Na Mueang and the restoration of Takua Pa

1777 (2320 BE) or during the town's revival in the reign of King Rama III (still debated)

Conflicting information

The origins of Wat Pathum Thara or Wat Na Mueang are told in two ways. Tesaban Mueang Takua Pa (Takua Pa Town Municipality) records the oral history that Lady Thao, daughter of Phraya Takua Pa (Muang), built the temple in 1777 (2320 BE). However, the ONEP old town database explains that after the old town was destroyed and abandoned during the Burmese war, the town's revival at Ban Talat Yai in the reign of King Rama III started with the construction of Wat Na Mueang, followed by the governor's residence nearby.

The big picture of Takua Pa is a riverine port town and a center for the tin trade. The town prospered from mining and maritime trade. Chinese laborers, especially Hokkien Chinese, settled and connected the culture with the Malay Peninsula. The temple likely served as the main religious site of the Talat Yai community and a landmark for the newly establishing town, but found sources do not identify a confirmed builder or its daily ritual roles in that era.

Before

From a water port to an old town district

1867·1956 (2410·2499 BE) and 1935 (2478 BE)

Verified

During the era of water trade, the temple was near areas with traffic and goods transport. Community sources indicate that a boiler in the temple grounds is a part of a steamboat that Phraya Senanuchit (Nuch na Nakhon) co-invested to buy in 1867 (2410 BE) to transport goods and suppress pirates, before the boat deteriorated and sank in the Takua Pa River. This evidence reflects trade networks but is not evidence that the temple operated mining businesses itself.

When the town center moved to Ban Yan Yao in 1913 (2456 BE) and waterways became shallow, Ban Talat Yai's economic role declined. The temple remained a religious site and community space. The temple's monks' living quarters were initially used as classrooms for Rongrian Tesaban Ban Si Takua Pa (Ban Si Takua Pa Municipal School) before the school moved to its current location in 1956 (2499 BE). The municipality notes that the southern vernacular ordination hall was registered as an ancient monument in 1935 (2478 BE), but the original announcement has not been checked in this research phase.

Now

A religious site in the Talat Yai community whose status requires on-site checking

2569 / 2026

Not verified

The verified information confirms the temple's location on Thanon Na Mueang in the Talat Yai community and confirms the value of the old ordination hall as a heritage of the old town. However, no official announcements or news in the last 5 years have been found that confirm restoration projects, regular activities, or direct visiting conditions of the temple.

A travel website states opening hours as 08:00·17:00 daily, yet there are no confirmed announcements from the temple or government agencies. It should be considered unverified information, and contacting the area before traveling is recommended. Access inside the ordination hall, the condition of the boiler, caretaking by the monastic community, and community activities in 2026 (2569 BE) remain issues that require field surveys.

FAQ

Opening hours

Not verified · The Love Thailand website states daily 08:00·17:00. Still no confirmed announcement from the temple.

What is the history of this place?

Municipal documents indicate that the ordination hall is of southern vernacular art and uses wooden pillars as its core. The municipality notes that Krom Sinlapakon (Fine Arts Department) registered the ordination hall as an ancient monument in 1935 (2478 BE); this research has not yet checked the original registration announcement.

What are the highlights to see?

The structural details mentioned in the municipal documents are suitable for observing vernacular craftsmanship; there is no information yet on whether it is fully visible from the outside. The boat part, which sources indicate is located in the temple grounds, is evidence connected to the town's water transport of goods. However, there is no latest condition check information in 2026 (2569 BE).
