Then

Guan Yu faith in the mining town: The origins of the South Market Shrine

1843 CE (an oral account; not verified)

Conflicting information

An account recorded by Gplace says that Hokkien Chinese people who came to mine in Khlong Pi community joined together to build a Guan Yu shrine, with Lim Bun Tuek involved in its founding, and began a nine-day, nine-night Vegetarian Festival ritual in 1843 CE. The same source spells personal names inconsistently, and no primary evidence has been found, so this should be read as an origin legend, not a verified year

At that time, Takua Pa was a coastal town growing through tin mining and Hokkien Chinese merchant networks. The shrine therefore likely served both as a home for the community's protective deity and as a ritual centre for miners and merchants. This is consistent with the municipality's description that the shrine arose from Hokkien Chinese faith and was the origin of Takua Pa's Vegetarian Festival, but the year and builder have not been verified in academic documents.

Before

From a community shrine to the South Market Shrine in the old town

B.E. 2445 (traditional account; not verified) to before B.E. 2567

Not verified

Gplace says the shrine was moved from Khlong Pi community to a permanent site at Ban Chae Lim in Talat Yai, and that funds were raised to buy a house in 1902 CE by Lim Eng Cheng, or Tao Kae Pho Daeng. However, no evidence has been found confirming the building date or transfer of ownership, so that year should not be treated as the construction year of the current shrine

The evidence found describes continuity in the site's role rather than a change of function: the shrine remains tied to Takua Pa's Vegetarian Festival, which has been passed down among Thai people of Chinese descent on the Andaman coast, and later became part of the Talat Yai old-town landscape. At the same time, no online evidence has been found confirming any restoration, abandonment, direct impact from the 2004 tsunami, or registration of this shrine as a historic site.

References · Gplace · ศูนย์มานุษยวิทยาสิรินธร (Princess Maha Chakri Sirindhorn Anthropology Centre): Takua Pa Town · ศูนย์มานุษยวิทยาสิรินธร (Princess Maha Chakri Sirindhorn Anthropology Centre): Talat Yai
Now

A shrine still breathing with the vegetarian festival

2569 / 2026

Verified

The latest news evidence found confirms the shrine's use as Talat Tai's vegetarian-festival shrine: on 2 October 2024, a Ko Teng pole-raising ceremony was held there to begin the Vegetarian Festival. The hanging of nine lanterns and continuing ceremonies show that the shrine remains a centre of community faith and shared activities, not merely an old building in the market area

For 2026, no source has confirmed opening hours, a custodian, admission fees, the building's latest condition, or a restoration project specifically for the shrine. Contact the community or municipality before travelling. Based on the information available, the shrine is near the junction of Thanon Udom Thara and Thanon Si Takua Pa in the Talat Yai area, and the confirmed activities are the Vegetarian Festival and the Ko Teng pole-raising ceremony.

References · Manager Online · ศูนย์มานุษยวิทยาสิรินธร (Princess Maha Chakri Sirindhorn Anthropology Centre): Talat Yai

FAQ

What is the history of this place?

The municipality states that the shrine grew from the faith of Hokkien Chinese people in Takua Pa, is hundreds of years old, and serves as a spiritual anchor through belief in Guan Yu and Guanyin. The municipality identifies this shrine as the origin of Takua Pa's Vegetarian Festival

What are the highlights to see?

The municipality lists important objects of worship at this shrine. Gplace says it has a palanquin used in processions. Check on site whether it is open for viewing on the day of travel

How the municipality document mentions this place

A shrine associated with belief in the deity Guan Yu and the trading community
