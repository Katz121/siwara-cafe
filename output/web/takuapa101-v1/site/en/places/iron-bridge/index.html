Then · Before · Now

Then

From a tin dredger to a workers' bridge

1960-1968 CE (2503-2511 BE)

Verified

The bridge's story began in Takua Pa during the tin-mining era, when the Boonsung dredging company stood on the opposite bank from the old market. The Takua Pa canal was more than 40 metres wide, so workers and residents had to row across, especially when floodwaters rose. The local database of Prince of Songkla University states that mining operator Juti Boonsung therefore first initiated a wooden bridge for walking and cycling

Later, in 1968 CE (2511 BE), steel from a decommissioned dredger was assembled into Khok Khanuen Iron Bridge, more than 200 metres long, across the Takua Pa River. The bridge was therefore not only industrial infrastructure, but also a shortcut for workers, a goods route, and a daily connection between the old market and the town's tin-driven mining area. Verifiable evidence confirms the mining and transport roles, but no contemporary document has been found directly linking this bridge to details about maritime trade, Hokkien Chinese people, or the town-governor system

Before

When the mines went quiet, the bridge became a trace of the town's mining past

After 1968 CE (2511 BE) and before 2026 CE (2569 BE)

Verified

When mining ended, the bridge did not disappear with the dredging plant. It changed from a work structure into a community route and evidence of the industrial landscape. ONEP sources state that the old dredging plant, preserved in its original condition, can still be seen from the bridge area, while contemporary tourism sources call the bridge a symbol of Takua Pa's mining-town past

Later, the bridge was described and presented as a tourist and photography spot, and it also appeared as a location in the film Wonderful Town (2007), according to an article tracking filming locations. However, this review found no evidence confirming when the mine closed, when major repairs took place, any direct impact from the 2004 tsunami, or registration as a historic site by the Fine Arts Department. These events should therefore not be added as facts

Now

A mining heritage that remains a passageway and destination

2569 / 2026

Verified

Today, Boonsung Iron Bridge still serves as a community route. Field information and reviews say that residents use it for travel and motorcycles pass through. At the same time, Takua Pa Municipality classifies it as a tourist attraction, and a TAT tourism source has current images of the site. The bridge is both functioning infrastructure and a landmark for walks, views, and photography

The latest evidence found is an NBT report dated 30 January 2026, which introduces the bridge as mining heritage associated with Takua Pa and says it was temporarily closed at that time to accommodate a foreign film shoot. In addition, a municipal budget document found includes items for maintaining and improving the bridge entrance area, but no document has been found confirming a restoration project for the structure itself. Opening and closing hours, admission fees, an activity-specific custodian, and the latest safety condition therefore still need to be checked on site

FAQ

Opening hours

Information not verified · Open to visitors all day (information from a secondary tourism source)

Is parking available?

Reviews state that roadside parking is available with limited space, but this is not verified by official sources

Getting there from Takua Pa Town

Travel along Rat Bamrung Road towards Takua Pa old town; the bridge is on the left, about 2 kilometres from the town centre.

What is the history of this place?

In 1968 (B.E. 2511), steel from a mining dredger was used to build the Khok Khanun Iron Bridge, more than 200 metres long, across the Takua Pa River. The initiative came from Juti Boonsung, owner of Juti Boonsung Mining Dredger Co., Ltd., founded in 1960 (B.E. 2503).

What are the highlights to see?

The bridge crosses the Takua Pa River, linking the main road area with the former mining plant. It uses steel from a decommissioned mining dredger, and the old plant is still visible at the far end. From the bridge, you can see green fields, trees, and mountain ranges, making it suitable for views and photography.
