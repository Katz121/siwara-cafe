Then · Before · Now

Then

A shrine from the Chinese community in a mining port

The construction year is unknown; it was probably built during the formation of Takua Pa's old-town community, but the year cannot yet be specified

Verified

Official evidence from Takua Pa Municipality identifies Bun Thao Kong A Shrine as the town's first shrine and places it in the old port district known as Chap Se or Chap Sao. This locates the shrine within Takua Pa's history of waterborne trade and old-market activity, but the documents found give neither a construction start year nor the name of an individual founder

The Talat Yai Community database identifies the builders as Chinese immigrants who came to Takua Pa to mine, mentioning groups such as Hokkien, Hainanese, and Cantonese. This presents the shrine as both a ritual site and an anchor for the workers and merchants of a mining town. However, there is not enough direct evidence to determine which group founded it or what specific role the shrine played in the port system

The municipality also records an incense burner “presented by King Rama VI”. This is evidence of a past relationship between the local shrine and the Siamese state, but the year, occasion, and provenance of the object are not specified

Before

An old shrine with gaps in its transition records

The year of restoration or any ownership change is unknown; activity records exist from at least 2021 (พ.ศ. 2564)

Verified

Among the verifiable public sources, no record has been found that the shrine was abandoned, changed hands, or restored in any particular year, and no ancient-monument registration announcement has been found for this shrine. Such details should therefore not be inferred from the context of the old town or other shrines

What can be verified is that in 2021 (พ.ศ. 2564), a video recorded the Sae Yit ceremony for Phra Hok Tek Jiah Sin or Pae Kong at Pun Thao Kong Am Shrine in Chap Se, while another video mentions the oil-bathing ceremony, Phra La ceremony, and bringing in the sacred fire. This shows that the shrine was still a ritual space for Takua Pa descendants at that time

As for whether the 2004 tsunami (พ.ศ. 2547) affected the building, and the effects of tin-mine closures and economic change in the town, no source has been found that addresses this shrine directly. This is recorded as a research gap, without concluding whether damage occurred

Now

Still alive as a community shrine, but 2026 status data is incomplete

2569 / 2026

Not verified

The latest evidence found does not verify opening hours, an official custodian, an admission fee, or the building's condition in 2026 (พ.ศ. 2569). It should therefore not be written that the shrine is open to tourists every day. What can be verified is that it is on Udom Thara Road in Talat Yai Community and has a history connected to the old port district of Chap Se

In terms of community use, a 2021 (พ.ศ. 2564) video shows Chinese ceremonies taking place and Takua Pa descendants returning to join the activities. The shrine therefore still appears to be a place of faith and shared community memory, but there is not enough public evidence to identify an annual event calendar or a management committee

No news from the past five years has been found confirming a restoration project, award, or development project specifically for this shrine, and no current image from an official agency with verifiable usage rights is available

FAQ

What is the history of this place?

Municipal documents state that Bun Thao Kong A Shrine was the first shrine in Takuapa and is located in the old port district known as Chap Se or Chap Sao. The municipality records that the shrine contains an incense burner presented by King Rama VI, but does not specify the year or occasion of the presentation.

What are the highlights to see?

A travel article states that the shrine's principal deity is Pun Thao Kong. This has not been verified by the shrine or an official agency. A sight in the area around the shrine, not inside it: the community database mentions a two-storey old building on Udom Thara Road in Chap Se

How the municipality document mentions this place

Documents mention the shrine in Takua Pa's old port district
