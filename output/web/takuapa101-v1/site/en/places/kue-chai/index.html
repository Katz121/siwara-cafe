Then · Before · Now

Then

From Mr. Ngo Chok's house to Takua Pa's first shrine

No verified construction year is available; some contemporary documents state that it is nearly 200 years old

Verified

The starting point with the clearest evidence is not a year, but a community story: Mr. Ngo Chok bought house number 87 on Sri Takua Pa Road and converted it into a shrine, driven by faith after praying for tin mining yields and fortune. Another document refers to its original name as 'Thian Wan Keng' and its legal name as 'Kue Chai Tung'.

References · สภาวัฒนธรรมตำบลตะกั่วป่า (Takua Pa Sub-district Cultural Council), Takua Pa Vegetarian Festival · สำนักงานนโยบายและแผนทรัพยากรธรรมชาติและสิ่งแวดล้อม (Office of Natural Resources and Environmental Policy and Planning)
Before

The shrine grew with the mining town and became a community ritual space

No year has been found for a change of ownership, restoration, or a tsunami event specifically verified for this site

Verified

The context of the shrine is tied to Hokkien Chinese who immigrated to mine tin in Takua Pa and brought deities from China to be enshrined. Local sources and travel media explain that the shrine is in the old market district, which has old buildings and a cultural street. It therefore serves as more than a place to pray: it is an anchor for the Chinese community and a space for the Vegetarian Festival.

References · สำนักงานนโยบายและแผนทรัพยากรธรรมชาติและสิ่งแวดล้อม (Office of Natural Resources and Environmental Policy and Planning) · Takua Pa Old Town boundary delineation project · Wanderlog (place information summary/user reviews)
Now

A northern shrine still alive during the Vegetarian Festival

2569 / 2026

Verified

The latest evidence found is NBT news published in May 2026 (2569 B.E.), which presents Kue Chai Tung Shrine, or 'Rong Phra Talat Nuea' (the northern market shrine), as a center of faith for Takua Pa people and states that it still preserves its original cultural identity. News of the 2023 festival also confirms that Kue Chai Tung Shrine participated in the Iu Keng ceremony, parading deities around the old town of Takua Pa.

References · NBT2HD · Innews

FAQ

How to dress

Dress politely when entering for worship.

What is the history of this place?

It is the first shrine in Takua Pa District, with deities invited from China to be enshrined here. According to Cultural Council documents, Ngo Chok bought house number 87 on Thanon Si Takua Pa and turned it into a shrine after praying for good mining yields and good fortune.

What are the highlights to see?

It houses Na Cha and other deities for worship. Community documents link the shrine to prayers for good ore and profitable mining. During the Vegetarian Festival, the shrine holds the Koi Han ceremony, a bridge-crossing ritual, and a sacred-thread tying ritual for good fortune.
