Then · Before · Now

Then

Wat Mai Kamphaeng in a mining and port town

Since 1847 (B.E. 2390)

Verified

This place began at Wat Sena Nuch Rangsan, formerly known by local residents as “Wat Mai Kamphaeng.” The temple register records its establishment in B.E. 2390 (1847), while local sources say it was built during the reign of King Rama III by Phraya Sena Nuchit (Nuch), governor of Takuapa.

At that time, Takuapa was an Andaman coastal port linking trade and tin resources. Various groups, especially workers and Chinese merchants, settled here, forming a market district and Chinese culture. The temple's Chinese-influenced ordination hall is therefore evidence of a multicultural town. For the museum seen today, no publicly available evidence identifies the year it was established or its direct founder.

References · Phang Nga Province temple registration report · Cultural environments for conservation (ONEP) · ศูนย์มานุษยวิทยาสิรินธร (Princess Maha Chakri Sirindhorn Anthropology Centre): Takua Pa Town
Before

From a temple and community to a museum of memories

No establishment year for the museum has been found; evidence shows its use in the old-town context before 2018 (B.E. 2561).

Verified

Over time, Wat Sena Nuch Rangsan has remained a religious site and community space. Meanwhile, Takuapa changed from a centre of tin mining and trade into a historic town preserving its heritage. Academic sources describe Takuapa Community Museum as a two-storey building displaying household items, equipment, and rare objects reflecting local life, including traces of the mining era.

The temple's ordination hall is registered as an ancient monument by the Fine Arts Department, but the sources verifiable this time do not clearly state the registration year. No evidence has been found that the museum building was abandoned, underwent major restoration, or was directly affected by the 2004 tsunami (B.E. 2547), so no conclusion is made beyond the evidence.

References · วารสารปาริชาต มหาวิทยาลัยทักษิณ (Parichart Journal, Thaksin University) · Cultural environments for conservation (ONEP) · สารสนเทศท้องถิ่นภาคใต้ฝั่งอันดามัน (Andaman Southern Thai Local Information)
Now

A small museum on the old town route

2569 / 2026

Verified

Contemporary sources call this place both “Takuapa Community Museum” and “Takuapa House Museum.” It is presented in the context of the old-town cultural street, displaying photographs, artefacts, and community stories alongside Sino-Portuguese buildings and old temples. Its role is therefore more that of a place for learning about the town's memories than a large museum with full visitor services.

However, no official announcement has been found confirming opening hours, admission fees, the direct custodian, the building's latest condition, or a museum-specific restoration project in 2026 (B.E. 2569). Old-town and cultural-street activities continue at municipal and community levels, but this should not be interpreted as evidence that the museum opens every day.

FAQ

How to dress

Modest attire is recommended when entering the temple grounds.

What is the history of this place?

Takuapa Community Museum is a two-storey building inside Wat Sena Nuch Rangsan. It collects household items and equipment reflecting life in Takuapa, including objects from the mining era. Wat Sena Nuch Rangsan was built by Phraya Sena Nuchit (Nuch), and its ordination hall shows Chinese architectural influence.

What are the highlights to see?

The community museum is inside Wat Sena Nuch Rangsan. It preserves mining-era objects and rare items reflecting local life. The large ordination hall is influenced by Chinese architecture.
