Takua Pa Stories · People and Flavors

Flavours of the Baba Community and the Old Market

Food serves as both household memories and evidence of cultural exchange · Read Takua Pa through the Baba cuisine, double-layered Tao Sor, and the Culture Street

Created by Baan Siwara Takua Pa · Updated 2026-09-07 · 7 min read

A town that was once a tin mining hub and a port on the trans-peninsular route like Takua Pa has left behind more than just traces of Sino-Portuguese buildings or the operating hours of local markets. It also hides its own stories in the flavours and the way of life of its people.

In official documents, Takua Pa appears as a community that grew from integrating external cultures. Each dish acts as a reflection of settlement migration, cross-cultural marriages, and the changes in commercial districts through different eras. The flavours of today are edible memories.

Takua Pa on the Baba Food Map

When discussing the food culture on the Andaman coast, the Department of Cultural Promotion officially listed Takua Pa as part of the Baba-Peranakan food communities since its registration as a national cultural heritage in 2013 (2556 BE). This inclusion positions Takua Pa within the network of overseas Chinese descendants who arrived to mine tin and settled down with the locals.

This knowledge of flavours and cooking methods did not originate from any single isolated group. Instead, it emerged from passing down memories and culinary techniques within families. It is about preserving the identity and taste from generation to generation, whether it is a savoury dish in a regular meal or sweets for important family rituals.

However, placing Takua Pa on this food culture map serves to explain the overall cultural context. It does not mean that every household or shop in Takua Pa shares the exact same recipe for Moo Hong (braised pork belly), O-tao (fried oyster with taro), or Ang Ku (red tortoise cake). The diversity within each household remains the heart of the local food's vitality.

Reading food menus or observing the local ingredients used in the market is a natural and gradual way of getting to know the way of life and flavours of Takua Pa.

Double-layered Tao Sor and the Town's Transformation

Among the frequently mentioned local foods, Tao Sor (flaky pastry) is a name that clearly appears in government records. The Phang Nga Provincial Tourism and Sports Office notes that Tao Sor is both a famous souvenir and an OTOP (One Tambon One Product) item of Takua Pa, describing it as similar to Chinese mooncakes but smaller, and often topped with sesame seeds.

The enduring presence of Tao Sor in Takua Pa was also recorded by the Food and Drug Administration during a field inspection in August 2025. It was documented that Tuang Rat shop continues the tradition of making Tao Sor using the traditional double-layered dough, an effort that earned them the FDA Quality Award for outstanding health products in the grassroots economy promotion category.

The story of Tao Sor helps us see how local sweets can develop and grow alongside the town's economy. At the same time, we cannot jump to the conclusion that Tao Sor originated in Takua Pa as the first in the world, or that there is only one monopolised original recipe here. This is because there is no historical evidence that clearly confirms its exact origin.

Origin and original recipe are not settled

Culture Street and the Old Commercial District

Stepping onto Si Takua Pa Road and Udom Thara Road, we are entering the former commercial centre of the tin mining era. Traces of this golden age are still visible through the architecture of shophouses with Straits colonial influences, featuring covered walkways at the front. Today, these streets have been repurposed into the Takua Pa Old Town Culture Street to draw people back to experience life in the old district.

The Department of Internal Trade's registry notes that this market is a gathering place for traditional sweets, local food, handicrafts, wickerwork, and cultural activities. The once-sleeping old streets are awakened every Sunday afternoon, creating a space where the younger generation and the original locals can meet and converse through shops and food.

However, the starting point of these activities still has information that is not entirely agreed upon. Some local news sources state that the retrospective event started in 2007, while official market registries list its founding and promotion in 2017. This discrepancy in information could be interpreted as referring to different milestones, between the community-initiated event and the official registration as a state-promoted market.

Starting year of the event has conflicting sources

Lan Long Market, A Meeting Point of Diversity

In addition to the walking street that opens once a week, Takua Pa also has a food hub ready to accommodate people every day, such as the Takua Pa Lan Long Food Centre. This place gathers various main dishes, ranging from rice and curry, made-to-order meals, Khanom Chin (fermented rice noodles), and noodles, to a complete selection of desserts and beverages.

These places are not just venues for buying and selling goods, but also serve as spaces for the townspeople to exchange conversations. Reading handwritten food menus or observing the local ingredients used in the market is a natural and gradual way of getting to know the way of life and flavours of Takua Pa.

As for information regarding the food centre's operating hours or the status of specific shops, data on the internet can be inaccurate and is not always updated in real-time. Asking locals on foot or checking directly with the vendors before your trip is a way to ensure you do not miss the chance to taste your favourite dishes.

Asking local people or checking directly with vendors before your trip can help you plan food stops according to the current situation.

Siwara Cafe in the Town's Storytelling System

If you visit Takua Pa today, you might see many coffee shops and cafes attempting to present the town's story through their drinks and decor. Siwara Cafe is one of them, and is also the creator of this Takua Pa 101 information. The website has specifically categorised the cafe under the beverage shop section.

This categorisation and explanation are done so that readers can clearly, transparently, and without confusion separate the role of the information creators from historical facts. As for operating hours, new menus, or prices, readers can check the latest details directly through the shop's communication channels.

The growth of coffee shops and sitting areas today is a reflection of building upon the drinking and eating culture of the past. It blends with the lifestyles of the younger generation and locals who participate in revitalising the old district, bringing it back to life.
