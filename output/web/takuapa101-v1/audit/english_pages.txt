Then · Before · Now
Then
From mining town donations to Takua Pa's first Chinese school
พ.ศ. 2463·2465 / 1920·1922 CE
Verified
Before it had a permanent building, Tao Ming School informally taught Chinese through private instruction. When the Private Schools Act of 1918 (พ.ศ. 2461) came into effect, the school formally declared its intention to be established in 1920 (พ.ศ. 2463), using the name “To Beng” in Hokkien pronunciation, before changing to “Tao Ming” in Mandarin pronunciation. The name conveys the idea of leading toward knowledge.
The surviving building was constructed in 1922 (พ.ศ. 2465) with funds collected from mining magnates and Chinese merchants in the old market at Yan Yao, as well as merchant networks from Ranong and Phuket. Another official source describes it as the work of a Chinese merchants' association that entered the tin-mining industry to provide Chinese-language education for Chinese children. The adapted Sino-Portuguese building with its corrugated-metal roof was therefore not only a school, but evidence that wealth from mining, trade, and Chinese networks was becoming a community institution.
At that time, Takua Pa was a trading and transport district connected to the river and sea. The town grew through tin, while Chinese people from Hokkien, Guangdong, and Hainan came as workers, merchants, and entrepreneurs. The school helped give the Chinese community in the flourishing mining town a linguistic and cultural foundation.

Before
When the mining town quieted down, the school paused, but the building still speaks
พ.ศ. 2520·2568 / 1977·2025 CE
Verified
Local sources state that Takua Pa's mines gradually closed from the 1970s, reducing the town's activity, while Tao Ming School ceased operations later. Verifiable official sources confirm only that “the school has been discontinued” and that the building is under the care of the Tao Ming School Foundation, Takua Pa. They do not specify the year it ceased operating or details of any change in ownership.
No readable evidence has been found confirming that the building was directly affected by the 2004 tsunami (พ.ศ. 2547) or that it underwent restoration in any particular year. It should therefore not be concluded that it was damaged or repaired because of that event, even though the Takua Pa area has sites connected with tsunami survivor assistance and the old town has regained a cultural-tourism role.
The old town boundary document by ONEP indicates the boundary passing through Tao Ming School, and the community database of the Sirindhorn Anthropology Centre classifies the building among the important evidence of the old town. However, this search has not yet found a Fine Arts Department announcement directly registering the “Tao Ming School Building”. Therefore, the conservation status of the area is separated from the registration status of the building.

Now
The original building remains in the old town, but it is no longer an active school
2569 / 2026
Verified
The currently verified status is that the school has closed down, leaving a historical building under the care of the Munlanithi Rongrian Tao Ming Takua Pa (Tao Ming Takua Pa School Foundation). Therefore, it should be understood that "visiting the building" is not the same as "visiting an operating school".
The building is located on Thanon Si Takua Pa in the old town area and is still used as a destination in tourism media by the Thetsaban Mueang Takua Pa (Takua Pa Town Municipality), the Kan Thongthiao Haeng Prathet Thai (Tourism Authority of Thailand), and old town activities, such as the cultural street held in the Thanon Si Takua Pa area. The information found verifies the context of activities in the area but it is not verified that activities are regularly held inside the building.
The condition verified from official sources is that the building remains and has architectural value in applied Sino-Portuguese style. As for opening hours, admission fees, regular caretakers, interior access, wheelchair accessibility, and the 2026 (2569 BE) restoration project, there is still no verifiable public information. Therefore, one should contact the caretaker or municipality before traveling.

Takua Pa Stories · About the Town
Takua Pa: The Town That Moved with the River
From an ancient settlement on the trans-peninsular route to a market and mining town · Verified facts and questions waiting to be answered.
Updated on 2026-09-07 · 6-minute read
Takuapa Riverwalk
Illustration · No licensed photo available for this subject yet
If you have a chance to take a walk in Takua Pa old town on a holiday afternoon, you will see old houses, long streets, and a quiet, charming atmosphere. But deep beneath the cracks in the old walls and the slow-flowing river, this town hides a story of major transformation.
Takua Pa was not located here from the beginning, and it did not always look like this. The town is a living entity that grew, expanded, and moved in rhythm with its resources and the river. From a stopover point on the trans-peninsular route, it became a bustling major market town, and later a town that had to find a new home when the river was no longer favorable.
We will take you to read Takua Pa through what remains: the governor's residence wall that tells a story of wealth, the old temple by the canal, and the river that was once its main artery. This is a town with more than one version of history.
The Ongoing Debate Over the Town's Name
Wat Kongkha Phimuk (the riverside temple)
Illustration
When speaking of Takua Pa, the first question many people wonder is where the town's name came from. Official sources and historians still offer different explanations, and to date, there is no evidence that can bring this to a single, settled conclusion.
The first explanation links the town's name to the word "Takola", which appears in ancient records as a major port on the trans-peninsular route. This word is interpreted as a possible root that gradually changed in pronunciation to the current name over several centuries.
Another explanation is based on local geography and resources. It suggests that the name comes from combining the word "Takua" (which means tin, a mineral abundantly found in the area) and "Pa" (which means forest, reflecting the rich natural environment surrounding the town).
Although documents from the Office of Natural Resources and Environmental Policy and Planning (ONEP) list both explanations as basic information, they clearly emphasize that there is no definitive evidence to confirm either one. The town's name remains the first mystery greeting visitors, leaving us to imagine the past through different explanations.
The origin of the town's name is not settled

The Governor's Residence Wall and Town Defense
Governor’s Residence Wall
Illustration
The image of Takua Pa's past wealth is inextricably linked to the tin mining business. One of the structures that most clearly reflects the power and context of that era is the Takua Pa governor's residence wall, which stands as a historical witness to this day.
Scholars have suggested, not settled, that this wall was built during the time of Phraya Senanuchit (Nuch Na Nakhon) while he was the governor between 1840 (2383 BE) and 1881. The wall surrounding the residence was designed as a defensive camp. During an era when the town faced conflicts among Chinese groups linked to the massive benefits from tin mining, the wall served as both a home for the ruler and a fortress to maintain peace.
The wall's structure is highly stable, constructed with layers of mortar mixed with gravel and sand. The base was designed to be wider than the wall itself. The parts that remain intact today reach a height of about 5 meters. Inside the wall, the area was divided into the front palace, the main hall, and the rear palace, with connecting doors well-proportioned throughout.
The significance of this governor's residence also appears in historical records when King Chulalongkorn (Rama V) came to inspect the residence on May 28, 1890 (109 RS or 2433 BE). During the excavation by the Fine Arts Department in 1999, Chinese ceramics from the Qing Dynasty and European ceramics were found, providing evidence of Takua Pa's international trade connections.
The construction year is suggested, not settled, based on the governor's tenure
A Town That Grew with the River
The Takua Pa River is not just a backdrop for the town, but the main artery that has nourished life and trade since the pioneering era. Official documents explain that Takua Pa old town is an ancient settlement located by the river, and it prospered as a trade center on the trans-peninsular route.
The bustling water trade and tin mining led to the expansion of an area called Talat Yai (the big market) during the reign of King Rama III, which became an important economic center. Near the hustle and bustle of trade, Wat Kongkha Phimuk (the town's temple) or Wat Kongkha, is situated on the west bank of the Takua Pa canal, serving as the spiritual center for the townspeople.
The history of Wat Kongkha reflects the complexity of the old town's information. The cultural environment data states that this temple was built in 1762, while the archaeological database uses 1936 as the construction year for the beautiful southern style ordination hall. This hall is decorated with stucco floral patterns and Buddha images, which are still preserved today.
The differences in these sets of numbers are something we often find in local history. Explaining the town's antiquity through official documents only provides an overview of the settlement in the entire area. It does not mean that every building, every bridge, or every location in the present town has the same age dating back to the town's founding era.
The age of the location and the age of the building might not be the same year
The Day the River Grew Shallow and the Town Had to Move
But nature never stays still, and the river is always ready to change. As time passed, the river that built the town began to change its role. ONEP reports note the end of the golden age of Talat Yai, which was primarily caused by geography and changes in resources.
Long and continuous tin mining caused a massive amount of sand and sediment to flow down and accumulate in the river. The Takua Pa River, which used to be deep enough for cargo ships and passenger boats to navigate easily, gradually became shallower until large-scale navigation declined and became difficult.
Eventually, when the river was no longer favorable as a center for trade and transportation, moving the town's administrative center was inevitable. Official reports state that Takua Pa had to move its town center to an area called Yan Yao in 1913.
That relocation marked the end of Talat Yai's prosperity as a center, turning this area into an old town stopped in time with the past. It left behind Sino-Portuguese architecture and traces of development for future generations to experience and study.
Reasons for the town relocation are based on ONEP reports
Reading the Town Today
Today, Takua Pa old town has come back to life in a different form from the past. The waterfront by the canal, which used to be a port for transporting tin, has now been revived into the area of the Takua Pa riverside market. It becomes lively again from Saturday afternoon to evening, filled with smiles, the aroma of local food, and the chatter of locals.
The governor's residence wall, once strong, is no longer used to defend against enemies. Instead, it has become a public space open to visitors and is often used as a venue for traditional festivals or major town events. Meanwhile, Wat Kongkha Phimuk continues to function as an active temple with residing monks. It is a peaceful place for meditation and a historical site that is still breathing.
If you have the chance to walk along the cultural street and the river, what surrounds you are not just stagnant ruins of the past, but evidence of adaptation for survival. The town that moved with the river back then continues to flow and change along with the people today.
Before fully believing any numbers or legends of the town, we would like to invite you to read and observe this town carefully. The history of Takua Pa is a charming large book. Some pages might be missing due to time, and some pages are still being written by all of us.

Restaurants and food
BAAN LAPP (Homey & Steak, Takua Pa)
A warm, homey restaurant and cafe with a Hidden Gem style, serving steak, pasta, and carefully prepared fusion dishes.
Open 11:00 - 21:00 (closed Wednesdays) · ~2.2 km from Siwara
From 17:30 onward, the lights create a pleasant atmosphere. It is suitable for a family dinner.

Kru Aew's Khanom Chin and Pork Satay (Thung Phra Pho)
A tucked-away shop in Thung Phra Pho serving tender, juicy charcoal-grilled pork satay marinated with spices, hot off the grill with boldly flavored southern Thai khanom chin and curry.
Open 12:00 - 18:00 (closed Thursdays) · ~2.4 km from Siwara
The pork satay is grilled fresh, skewer by skewer. A large set of 20 to 30 skewers is recommended with the khanom chin.
Navigate ↗
Phang Nga Noodles, Khwang Market
Springy, tender handmade egg noodles made with an old Hokkien recipe, tossed with fragrant fried garlic oil and large pieces of crispy pork lard, for a balanced, mellow flavor.
Open 09:00 - 15:30 (closed Tuesdays) · ~0.6 km from Siwara
Try the "special dry tom yum noodles with crispy pork lard". Toss well and enjoy without adding seasoning.
Navigate ↗
Pa Da Hokkien Noodles (Thanon Si Takua Pa)
A long-running Hokkien noodle shop on Thanon Si Takua Pa. The springy noodles are stir-fried with a fragrant wok-charred aroma, plenty of seafood, and a runny egg.
Open 07:00 - 17:00 (daily) · ~0.7 km from Siwara
Try the "Hokkien noodles stir-fried with a runny egg". Break the yolk and mix it into the hot noodles for a rich, savory taste.
Navigate ↗
Bangdeen Chicken Biryani & Curry Roti (Michelin Guide)
A well-known Muslim breakfast restaurant recognized by the Michelin Guide. Fragrant, tender spiced chicken biryani is served with a bold dipping sauce, along with soft roti and chicken massaman curry.
Open 06:00 - 13:30 (daily) · ~2.8 km from Siwara
The roti dough is freshly kneaded and fried until crisp yet soft. Try the massaman curry roti with hot pulled tea.
Navigate ↗
Black Crab Restaurant (Bang Nai Si)
A tucked-away restaurant beside a mangrove canal in Bang Nai Si, serving fresh wild black crab with firm, intensely sweet meat and boldly seasoned local-style dishes.
Open 10:00 - 21:00 (daily) · ~3.2 km from Siwara
Steamed black crab and crab fried rice are the highlights. Call ahead to check whether fresh ingredients are available, or go before evening.
Navigate ↗
Kae Pad Thai (Takua Pa Old Market)
A popular Pad Thai shop in the old town area. Chewy noodles stir-fried in a rich traditional tamarind sauce, with large, fresh, and bouncy shrimp.
Open 16:30 to 21:30 (daily) · ~0.6 km from Siwara
Fresh shrimp Pad Thai wrapped in egg is the specialty. Squeeze fresh lime over it and eat with banana blossom and pennywort. Flavorful without extra seasoning.
Navigate ↗
Jae Uan Talay Sod (Baan Nam Kem)
A renowned seafood restaurant in the Baan Nam Kem area. Fresh ingredients delivered directly from fishing boats daily. Spicy seafood dip made with real lime. Evening seaside atmosphere.
Hours: 10:30 to 21:30 (Daily) · ~11.5 km from Siwara
Great for dinner. Large tiger prawns with firm, sweet meat paired with spicy seafood dip.

Stops and Attractions
Thanon Si Takua Pa & Old Building Street Art
An old shophouse district with arched walkways (Five-Foot Way) featuring street art that tells the stories of the Baba people and the town's tin mining history.
Hours: Open all day (09:00 to 17:00 recommended) · ~0.7 km from Siwara
You can easily walk through various alleys connecting to Tao Sor pastry shops and old town eateries.
Navigate ↗
Tam Nang Waterfall (Si Phang Nga National Park)
A large waterfall in the middle of a lush rainforest in Si Phang-nga National Park. The water cascades down a 63-meter high cliff into a clear pool where Mahseer barbs swim.
Hours: 08:30 to 16:30 (Daily) · ~21.8 km from Siwara
Between 10:00 and 14:00, the sunlight hits the water surface beautifully. There is a shaded resting spot under large trees.
Navigate ↗
Boonsung Iron Bridge (Khok Khanun Bridge)
A historical landmark from the prosperous tin mining era. Built from the steel parts of an old tin dredger, elegantly spanning the Takua Pa River.
Hours: Open 24 hours (16:30 to 18:30 recommended) · ~2.3 km from Siwara
Between 17:00 and 18:30, the sunset light shines through the steel bridge structure, turning it into a beautiful orange. Great for taking photos.
Navigate ↗
Lad Yai, the old town cultural walking street
A classic historical walking street. Thanon Si Takua Pa is closed to traffic so locals can set up stalls selling traditional food, local snacks, and crafts under Sino-Portuguese style lighting.
Hours: Sundays only 15:30 to 20:30 · ~0.7 km from Siwara
Open only on Sundays. Arriving around 17:00 is recommended when the orange sunlight beautifully lights up the classic old buildings for photos.
Navigate ↗
Souvenirs
Tao Sor Tuang Rat (100-year-old original recipe)
A top souvenir of Takua Pa. Thin crispy crust with dense mung bean filling, scented with traditional candle smoke. The recipe has been passed down through generations.
Hours: 08:00 to 17:00 (Daily) · ~0.6 km from Siwara
The salted egg yolk filling is their signature. Best bought fresh out of the oven. Can be kept for 7 to 10 days.
